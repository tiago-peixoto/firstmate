  Tides have a long-term effect as well. Friction from tidal motion slowly drains Earth's rotational energy, lengthening the day by about two milliseconds per century. That lost angular momentum is
  passed to the Moon, which drifts about 3.8 centimeters farther from Earth each year. Hundreds of millions of years ago, days were shorter and the Moon loomed larger in the sky.
  The tides at the edge of a beach are therefore part of a process that has been shaping Earth and its Moon for billions of years.
